# Camara v. Municipal Court, 387 U.S. 523 (1967) 案例分析：检查员进住宅也要先拿令状

> 分工说明：本篇管 Camara 案本身——案情、判决与射程。仓内《行政搜查与检查令备考核心》管答题框架，本篇只拆这一案；同题常识篇管检查员敲门时的日常应对。事实依据：Camara v. Municipal Court of the City and County of San Francisco, 387 U.S. 523 (1967)；See v. City of Seattle, 387 U.S. 541 (1967)；Frank v. Maryland, 359 U.S. 360 (1959)。

## 一、案情：旧金山检查员敲门，住户关门吃官司

### 中文
旧金山《住宅法典》第 503 条授权市公共卫生局的检查员进入住宅检查违规，第 507 条把拒绝检查员入户定为轻罪。Roland Camara 住在一栋楼的底层，检查员怀疑那套单元的居住用途没有许可证，上门要求进屋查看。他拒绝入户，也拿不出对方有令状，随后被控违反第 507 条。他在诉讼里直接挑战第 503 条本身：政府人员没有令状就想进住宅，住户拒绝反倒成罪，这与第四修正案对不上。加州法院维持了检控，案件打到联邦最高法院。案子看着小——一次没让进的门——问的却是大问题：行政检查算不算第四修正案说的搜查，进老百姓的家要不要先过法官这一关。

### English
Section 503 of the San Francisco Housing Code authorized city health inspectors to enter dwellings to check for code violations, and section 507 made refusing an inspector a misdemeanor. Roland Camara lived on the ground floor of a building; an inspector who suspected an unpermitted residential use asked to come in. Camara refused entry without a warrant and was charged under section 507. He attacked section 503 itself: if the government may enter a home without a warrant, punishing the occupant for saying no cannot be squared with the Fourth Amendment. California courts let the prosecution stand, and the case reached the U.S. Supreme Court — a small refused knock posing a large question: is an administrative inspection a Fourth Amendment search, and must the government see a judge before entering a home?

Roland Camara vivía en la planta baja de un edificio; un inspector que sospechaba un uso residencial sin permiso pidió entrar. Camara se negó sin orden judicial y fue acusado bajo la sección 507. Atacó la propia sección 503: si el gobierno puede entrar en un hogar sin orden, castigar al ocupante por negarse no encaja con la Cuarta Enmienda. Los tribunales de California mantuvieron la acusación y el caso llegó a la Corte Suprema de EE. ## 二、判决：1967 年 6 月 5 日，6 比 3，住宅检查要令状

### 中文
Camara v. Municipal Court of the City and County of San Francisco, 387 U.S. 523 (1967)（美国联邦最高法院，1967 年 6 月 5 日判决）以 6 比 3 撤销对 Camara 的定罪，White 大法官执笔多数意见。判决做了两件事。第一，明确行政检查也是第四修正案意义上的搜查：检查员进住宅看违规，与警察进住宅找证据，在住户的隐私这一侧没有本质区别，所以默认规则相同——先拿令状。第二，公开推翻八年前的 Frank v. Maryland, 359 U.S. 360 (1959)：那一案曾以 5 比 4 放行无令住宅健康检查，Camara 说它站不住了（1967 减 1959 等于 8，本地复算一致）。同日姊妹案 See v. City of Seattle, 387 U.S. 541 (1967) 把同一规则推到商业场所：西雅图仓库老板拒绝消防检查员无令入内，同样不能定罪。住宅与商铺同一天拿到令状保护，只是后文会看到，两边的保护强度并不完全同轨。

### English
Camara v. Municipal Court of the City and County of San Francisco, 387 U.S. 523 (1967) (U.S. Supreme Court, decided June 5, 1967) reversed Camara's conviction 6 to 3, in an opinion by Justice White. The decision did two things. First, it held that an administrative inspection is a search within the Fourth Amendment: an inspector entering a home to look for violations is, from the occupant's side of the door, not fundamentally different from an officer entering to look for evidence, so the default rule is the same — get a warrant first. Second, it expressly overruled Frank v. Maryland, 359 U.S. 360 (1959), which eight years earlier (1967 − 1959 = 8, verified by local recomputation) had upheld warrantless home health inspections 5 to 4. The same-day companion case, See v. City of Seattle, 387 U.S. 541 (1967), carried the rule to commercial premises: a Seattle warehouse owner who refused a fire inspector warrantless entry likewise could not be convicted. Homes and businesses received warrant protection on the same day, though, as later cases show, not at identical strength.

### Español (初译待校)
Camara v. Municipal Court of the City and County of San Francisco, 387 U.S. 523 (1967) (Corte Suprema de EE. UU., 5 de junio de 1967) revocó la condena de Camara por 6 a 3, con opinión del juez White. La sentencia hizo dos cosas. Primero, declaró que la inspección administrativa es un registro en el sentido de la Cuarta Enmienda: un inspector que entra en un hogar a buscar infracciones no difiere en lo esencial, desde el lado del ocupante, de un policía que entra a buscar pruebas, así que la regla por defecto es la misma: obtener primero una orden. Segundo, revocó expresamente Frank v. Maryland, 359 U.S. 360 (1959), que ocho años antes (1967 − 1959 = 8, verificado por recálculo local) había avalado por 5 a 4 las inspecciones sanitarias domiciliarias sin orden. El caso gemelo del mismo día, See v. City of Seattle, 387 U.S. 541 (1967), llevó la regla a los locales comerciales: el dueño de un almacén de Seattle que rechazó la entrada sin orden de un inspector de bomberos tampoco podía ser condenado. Hogares y negocios recibieron protección el mismo día, aunque, como mostrarían casos posteriores, no con idéntica intensidad. (初译待校)

## 三、理由：令状还在，相当理由换成行政标准

### 中文
Camara 最要紧的手艺，是没有把刑事搜查那套相当理由原样搬过来。刑事令状要指向具体的人、具体的赃物；住宅检查常常是整片社区按计划轮查，根本说不出哪一户有问题。法院于是把检查令状的相当理由重新定义：不需要证明这一户确有违规，合理的立法或行政标准就能支撑令状——楼龄、片区整体状况、距上次检查过了多久、检查计划本身是否中立而普遍适用。法官签这种区域令状，审的不是这一户可疑不可疑，而是整个检查方案像不像一场公平的例行公事。法院的平衡也很直白：例行住宅检查对隐私的侵扰有限，住户真正需要的是一个中立的司法官在门口纠纷发生之前先看一眼依据，而不是把同意与否的争吵留给住户和检查员在门口解决。拒绝无令入户因此不能入罪——这正是 Camara 本人获胜的那一点。

### English
Camara's most important craft was refusing to copy criminal probable cause wholesale. A criminal warrant points to a specific person and specific contraband; housing inspections often sweep a whole area on a schedule, with nothing particular known about any one home. The Court therefore redefined probable cause for inspection warrants: no proof of a violation in the particular dwelling is required, because reasonable legislative or administrative standards can supply it — the age of the building, conditions in the area, the time elapsed since the last inspection, and whether the inspection plan is neutral and generally applied. A judge issuing such an area warrant reviews not whether this household looks suspicious, but whether the whole program looks like a fair routine. The balance was stated plainly: routine home inspections intrude only modestly, and what the occupant really needs is a neutral magistrate reviewing the basis before a doorstep dispute, rather than leaving the argument to the occupant and the inspector at the door. Refusing warrantless entry therefore cannot be a crime — the very point on which Camara himself won.

### Español (初译待校)
La aportación más importante de Camara fue no copiar sin más la causa probable penal. Una orden penal apunta a una persona y un contrabando concretos; las inspecciones de vivienda suelen recorrer zonas enteras según un plan, sin saber nada particular de cada casa. La Corte redefinió así la causa probable de la orden de inspección: no hace falta probar una infracción en esa vivienda, porque estándares legislativos o administrativos razonables pueden sostenerla — antigüedad del edificio, condiciones de la zona, tiempo desde la última inspección y si el plan es neutral y de aplicación general. El juez que firma esa orden de área no examina si ese hogar parece sospechoso, sino si todo el programa parece una rutina justa. El equilibrio se dijo sin rodeos: la inspección rutinaria invade poco, y lo que el ocupante necesita es un juez neutral que revise la base antes de la discusión en la puerta, no discutir el consentimiento con el inspector en el umbral. Negarse a la entrada sin orden no puede ser delito: ese fue exactamente el punto que ganó Camara. (初译待校)

## 四、射程与加州坐标：Barlow's 之后的三扇侧门

### 中文

### English
Eleven years after Camara, Marshall v. Barlow's, Inc., 436 U.S. 307 (1978) carried the rule into federal workplace enforcement: section 8(a) of the Occupational Safety and Health Act, 29 U.S.C. section 657(a), which authorized OSHA inspectors to enter any workplace without a warrant, was held unconstitutional in that respect, while an inspection plan or specific information about violations can support a warrant (1978 − 1967 = 11). But a warrant is not the only door: voluntary consent still lets an inspection proceed; emergencies such as fire or collapse do not wait for paper; and a closely regulated industry exception covers businesses like liquor, firearms, mining and auto dismantling, which under the three requirements of New York v. Burger, 482 U.S. 691 (1987) — a substantial government interest, inspections necessary to the scheme, and a statute definite enough in time, place and scope to substitute for a warrant — may be inspected without one. The contrast case is Wyman v. James, 400 U.S. 309 (1971), which treated a welfare home visit as not a search at all and remains the line's most contested exception. California coordinates: article I, section 13 of the California Constitution runs parallel to the Fourth Amendment, and inspection-warrant procedure is codified at Code of Civil Procedure sections 1822.50 and following, issued by a judge on administrative standards and stating the place and purpose of the inspection. Camara was itself a California case: the line began at one San Francisco door and now stands 59 years old (2026 − 1967 = 59).
